But one development from Friday's tape deserves a clearer frame than the Saturday review gave it, because it is the deepest of the three forces now pressing on the AI complex:

  • The OpenAI IPO delay — the AI-capex-financing fear, named. A New York Times report says OpenAI's advisors laid out two paths: go public this year below $1T, or wait until 2027 to chase a ~$1T valuation — and Sam Altman reportedly rejected any cut to the trillion-dollar target, i.e. the company leans toward delaying. The numbers underneath are the point: OpenAI has signalled something on the order of ~$600B of targeted future compute capacity against a reported ~$25B of annualized revenue, and it is not yet profitable. That reframes the whole AI-infra build as tracing back to a small group of capital-hungry, still-private, unprofitable buyers — and it crept the "bubble" question back into Friday's tape (TheStreet flagged a reported OpenAI IPO delay as a driver of the 26 June tech sell-off).
  • This is the same overhang that has dogged ORCL since 10 June. Oracle's record Q4 (OCI +93%, RPO $638B) was sold on a $40B raise and a −$23.7B free-cash-flow capex burn — the market punishing the financing of AI infrastructure, not the demand. The OpenAI story is that same fear at the source of the demand curve. It is why ORCL keeps making new lows ($148.53) even as the fundamentals hold.
  • It stacks with — does not replace — Saturday's two frames. The confirmed-hawkish Fed (dot plot flipped to a 2026 hike, Sept-hike odds ~62–63%, 10-yr 4.38%) raises the discount rate on every long-duration AI dollar; the "RAMageddon" double-edge lifts the memory makers while squeezing the Mag7 device makers; and the OpenAI-financing question asks whether the end demand can ever earn its return. Three different pressures, one direction.

The disinflationary offset still stands. Brent ~$73 (−10% on the week, the lowest since late February) on US–Iran peace progress — Gulf exports back to ~75% of prewar levels, Saudi loading at Ras Tanura, Hormuz open — is the one macro force pushing against the hawkish Fed. Lower energy is disinflationary and a consumer tailwind; if it bends the September-hike odds, it is the cleanest path to the up-branch.

The response is unchanged because nothing traded: keep the rate-insensitive power/uranium sleeve (TLN/CEG/CCJ) and the LLY defensive anchor as ballast, hold the AI-infra winners (MU/NVDA/TSM) but with no chase, and let QCOM's fresh Investor-Day catalyst work. 10 KEEP, 0 swaps.
