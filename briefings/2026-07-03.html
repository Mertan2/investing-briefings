The two binaries split — cold jobs + a Tesla blowout, but chips kept selling: a clean rotation

Yesterday was the double-binary day, and it did not resolve to a single vector — it split, and the split is the whole story:

  • June payrolls came in cold — the up-branch for rates fired. The economy added just +57,000 jobs in June (vs ~115k consensus, and down from a downward-revised +129k in May), with April and May revised down a combined ~74k. Unemployment actually fell to 4.2% (a one-year low, on a shrinking labor force). The read-through was immediate: per CME FedWatch, September-hike odds dropped from ~65% to ~50% in the minutes after the print, a July hike became "difficult to envision" (BMO), and the 2-year yield eased. The hawkish-Fed cloud that has hung over this book for weeks lifted materially — though core PCE at 3.4% is the standing caveat (weak labor vs sticky inflation is the Fed's dilemma, not a green light to cut).
  • Tesla printed a blowout — 480,126 deliveries. Q2 deliveries of 480,126 smashed the ~406k IR / ~396.5k Bloomberg consensus and beat even the highest Street estimate (Barclays 418k) by ~60k — up +25% YoY, Tesla's best-ever second quarter, ending two years of delivery declines (European recovery + Model refresh). The $380 stop stays voided; the print extends the reclaim above $400 into the 22 July financials.
  • But the chip complex kept selling — a valuation de-rate, not a rate scare. Even with the dovish jobs print, memory/AI-hardware extended its rout: Micron fell to ~$978 (down ~21% over two sessions off the $1,145 ATH, now testing support with "$850" being floated), NVDA slipped ~2.5% to ~$193, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index shed roughly 5% on the day. The driver isn't the Fed — it's AI-valuation / memory-glut fear after months of parabolic gains. That's the tell: this is idiosyncratic to the crowded AI trade, not a macro risk-off.
  • So the tape rotated — and the Dow closed at a record. Money left the parabolic AI/chip names and went into rate-sensitive, old-economy value: the Dow finished at an all-time high 52,900.07 (+1.1%), the S&P was ~flat (tech drag erased the gains), and the Nasdaq fell −0.8%. A cleaner rotation is hard to draw.

This is exactly the tape the book was hedged for. For weeks the discipline has been: own the AI-infra winners but hold a rate-insensitive power/uranium + defensive ballast (TLN/CEG/CCJ/LLY/ADI) against the one cloud — rates — that jobs week would test. Jobs week tested it, the print came dovish, and the ballast is now the part of the book the rotation rewards. The response is therefore the simplest it's been in a while: 10 KEEP. No name broke its thesis; the AI/chip cohort (MU/NVDA/TSM/VRT/AVGO) is de-rating on valuation (own it, don't chase the falling knife), the ballast leads, and AVGO stays at Medium on probation because its gate — a clean close above $400 — still isn't met (~$370). The two deepened dislocations to watch are CEG (a fresh 52-week low ~$239) and CCJ (~$95, now well under the $110 add zone).
