Wednesday's ADP print had signalled it — 44,000 private jobs against 95,000 the prior month — and the book noted that number without weighting it. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%, which is the same participation-driven arithmetic that flattered June, and is why the headline rate is the wrong number to read this cycle. Treasuries rallied hard: 10-year 4.621% (−4bp), 2-year 4.176% (−6bp+). Equity futures, which had been up 0.2–0.6% before the release, extended into the print as hike risk came off.

The Fed pricing is genuinely unsettled, and this book is not going to pretend otherwise. One feed reads a 55% probability of a September hike, down from 63% a week ago. Another reads ~93% probability of no hike at all. Those are not reconcilable, and averaging them would manufacture a precision that does not exist. What is not in dispute: hike odds fell materially within the hour, and "jumbo cut" commentary appeared in the same window. The September meeting is 15–16 September. CPI and the July FOMC minutes land next week.

Thursday's tape, before any of that: Dow 53,870, −479 points (−0.9%), off Wednesday's record; S&P 500 −0.2%; Nasdaq −0.1%. Eight of eleven S&P sectors closed lower and the worst three were industrials, real estate and materials — a rate-and-oil day, not an AI day. Yields rose Thursday and fell Friday, which is the whole story of the last 24 hours in one line.

And oil went the other way for a reason that matters. Iran's state news agency Fars published an initial draft plan for the Strait of Hormuz that would bar US and Israeli vessels entirely and require countries Tehran deems hostile to pay compensation before transit. The US position is unrestricted passage. Brent settled $82.49, +3.8% on Thursday and is $81.67 (−0.99%) as this is written. The distance between "Iran and Oman have agreed a route" and "the strait reopens" turns out to be the entire text of the agreement — which is precisely the risk this book's own Brent rule was too fast to price.
