The regime changed: from the Moonshot scare to the free-cash-flow revolt

The last briefing framed Wednesday 22 July as the fork. It was — but it broke in a direction neither branch anticipated. Alphabet did not flinch. It doubled down, and got punished for it. That is a different market than the one the book was positioned for, and it deserves an honest re-score:

  • Alphabet crushed the capex question — and the stock fell anyway. 2026 capex guidance raised to $195–205B from $180–190B (the Street modelled ~$175–185B); Q2 capex alone was $44.9B, roughly double a year ago, ~60% servers / 40% data centres. Cloud revenue +82% YoY to $24.8B; backlog up $50B in a single quarter to $514B. Revenue $119.8B. This is total refutation of the "less compute needed" fear. And it produced −$5.9B free cash flow — the first negative FCF quarter since the 2004 IPO — so shares fell ~3.7% after hours and kept going.
  • The market's fear inverted. Two weeks ago the risk was that hyperscalers would cut spending. Now the risk is that they won't. Hyperscalers have moved from nearly self-funded capex to raising external capital at scale — incremental annual debt rose from ~9% of capex in FY24 to ~32% by mid-2026. The Mag7 lost roughly $890B in market value on the week, led by Alphabet and Tesla. The rotation is explicit: out of those writing the checks, into those receiving them.
  • Nvidia is now on the wrong side of that trade. NVDA fell 5.0% Monday to $196.51 — not on demand, but on circular-financing fear: the WSJ reported Nvidia is in talks to guarantee up to $250B of financing for OpenAI's data-centre buildout, on top of a $500B+ AI cooperation pact with SK Group. The question investors are now asking is whether that is customer-backed demand or supplier-funded demand. That is a genuinely new overhang, and it earns a downgrade.
  • The Iran thread reversed outright — and the war sleeve's catalyst is spent. The US paused strikes at Iran's request (Trump warning they resume absent a new deal); Iran says no negotiations are currently under way. Brent fell 11.3% to $85.87 — the biggest single-day drop since 8 April — after reaching ~$102 last week. XOM ran to $156.83 (a six-day rally, RSI overbought, avg PT only ~$163) and RTX printed a 52-week high at ~$212 on Monday. Both were bought for an escalation that has now paused at the exact moment they hit their targets. That is the textbook "catalyst spent" replace condition.
  • Micron reclaimed its base — the one clean upgrade. MU trades ~$900.20, back above the ~$880 base it lost on 17 July. That was the stated re-upgrade trigger and it fired. It did so despite a genuine new bear headline — China's CXMT completed an $8.6B IPO and rose 466% on debut — because CXMT remains at least a generation behind on HBM, where demand still outstrips supply.
